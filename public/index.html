Before you dive in

This tool only does the math — real SEC filings, real prices, real analyst consensus, run through a discounted cash flow model. It has no idea what happened in the news this morning, no sense of momentum, hype, or sentiment, and can't read a product launch, a lawsuit, or an earnings call.

It's one disciplined input, not the whole picture. It will not always agree with the market, and that's not automatically a bug — treat every number here as a starting point for your own thinking, not a verdict.

The math behind it

🏈 The playbook, spelled out — five steps from "cash the business makes" to "what a share is worth."
1. Cash flow. We use unlevered free cash flow (FCFF) — cash generated from operations, after tax and after reinvestment (CapEx), before any financing costs. It's what the business actually throws off regardless of how it's funded.
2. Growth. How fast that cash flow grows for the next several years — Wall Street's own long-term consensus estimate when available (most accurate, forward-looking), a historical-filings trend otherwise.
3. Discount rate (WACC). A dollar next year is worth less than a dollar today, and worth even less if the business is risky. WACC blends the return equity investors require (CAPM: risk-free rate + company-specific risk × market risk premium) with the cost of debt, weighted by how the company is actually financed.
4. Terminal value. Nobody can forecast forever, so after the explicit window we assume growth settles into a stable, roughly GDP-like pace forever, and value that entire future as one lump sum (the Gordon Growth formula) — discounted back to today just like every other year.
5. Per share. Add up all those discounted cash flows plus the discounted terminal value (enterprise value), subtract net debt (equity value), divide by shares outstanding. Compare that to today's price — that's the whole verdict.
